A friends-only project · Pulpit

Every General Conference talk, in one place.

From 1830 to today — gathered, sourced to the oldest copy we can find, and made readable by both people and their AI. I could use your help.

Why this doesn't exist yet

The talks are scattered everywhere.

The Church's website only goes back to 1971. Everything before that is spread across old scanned Conference Reports, the Journal of Discourses, the Joseph Smith Papers, old Deseret News pages, and library archives — mixed in with mountains of other material. There is no single, trustworthy place to read it all.

The mess today

  • Modern talks: easy.
  • 1942–1970: one paywalled-ish index.
  • Before 1942: scattered scans, no good search.
  • 1830s–1890s: buried in giant old volumes.

What we're building

  • One library, every talk.
  • Each one traced to its best, oldest source.
  • A quality grade on every transcript.
  • Readable by you and your AI.

The part I'm genuinely excited about

You can point your own AI at the whole thing.

This isn't another chatbot that makes things up. We're storing every talk as a clean, plain text file with its source attached — the format AI assistants read best. Then we plug your AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — whatever you use) directly into the library. It reads the real talks, finds what you're looking for across 170 years, and shows you exactly where each quote came from.

Why people will trust it

Every transcript gets a sourcing grade.

The whole point is honesty about how good our copy is. Modern talks are word-perfect. A sermon from the 1850s, taken down in shorthand by a reporter, is not — and we say so, out loud, instead of pretending.

VerbatimOfficial, word-for-word (modern era).
Authoritative printAs published in the official Conference Report.
Contemporaneous reportTaken down at the time — e.g. shorthand. Honest caveats attached.
ReconstructedAssembled from more than one partial witness.
FragmentaryOnly pieces survive. Flagged clearly.

Where you come in

What helping actually looks like.

This is a big pile of talks and I can't do it alone. The good news: your AI does most of the grunt work, and you stay in the driver's seat. Three steps.

Claim a conference (or a decade you're curious about)

You grab a chunk so two people don't duplicate work. I'll point you to exactly where those talks live — which scans, which volumes, which archive.

Let your AI do the heavy lifting — you check it

Your AI pulls the old scan and cleans the text into a readable transcript. Your job is to glance it against the original and fix anything obviously wrong. Far faster than typing it out.

Submit it

Send it my way. I give every talk a final review and assign its sourcing grade before it goes into the library. You get credit for the talks you bring in.

All you need

  • An AI assistant you're comfortable using (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — any).
  • A bit of interest in Church history. No coding, ever.
  • A few hours when you have them. An afternoon can net a whole conference.

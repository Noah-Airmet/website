Latter-day Saint theology

Becoming Like God

A beginner-friendly guide to what was actually taught, when it was taught, and how early Latter-day Saint ideas about exaltation developed from D&C 76 to D&C 132, King Follett, the Sermon in the Grove, Brigham Young, and beyond.

The short version: Early Latter-day Saint teaching about exaltation developed in stages. D&C 76 teaches that the exalted become "gods" as children and heirs of God. D&C 132 ties godhood to sealing, eternal marriage, and "continuation of the seeds." King Follett teaches that God himself progressed to godhood and that human spirits/intelligences are uncreated. The Sermon in the Grove most clearly extends this upward into a chain or hierarchy of divine beings. Brigham Young, Orson Pratt, and later writers then systematized heavenly parents, spirit birth, and eternal increase.
Canon first D&C 76, 93, and 132 are scripture for Latter-day Saints.
Sermons second King Follett and the Sermon in the Grove are Joseph Smith sermons, influential but not canon.
Later theology third Brigham Young, the Pratts, and Roberts explain and expand; they are not all saying the same thing.
Mechanics are open Modern official teaching affirms divine potential and heavenly parents but avoids many old speculative details.

Development Timeline

1832

D&C 76: Deification as Inheritance

canon

D&C 76 is the first major Restoration text to describe exalted people in divine terms. Those who receive the celestial fullness are called "gods, even the sons of God." They receive all things, dwell with God and Christ, and are made equal in power, might, and dominion.

Key wording

"they are gods, even the sons of God"

D&C 76:58; see also 76:95

Takeaway: exalted humans inherit divine glory as children and heirs of God through Christ.

Does not yet teach

Eternal marriage, heavenly parents, spirit birth, or an infinite regress of gods.

Development step

The basic deification claim is already present early: salvation's highest form is becoming like God, not merely living near God.

1833

D&C 93: The Uncreated Intelligence Constraint

canon

D&C 93 says intelligence "was not created or made, neither indeed can be." This becomes important later because it limits how literally one can say God or exalted beings "create" human spirits.

Key wording

"was not created or made, neither indeed can be"

D&C 93:29

Takeaway: something fundamental about human beings is eternal and uncreated.

Does not yet teach

A detailed model of intelligence, spirit body, birth, or premortal embodiment.

Development step

This creates the later problem: if spirits are uncreated, what does "spirit birth" mean?

1843

D&C 132: Exaltation as Sealed, Eternal Increase

canon

D&C 132 ties exaltation to covenant sealing. A marriage sealed by proper authority and ratified by the Holy Spirit of promise can remain after death. The exalted receive "a continuation of the seeds forever and ever"; then "shall they be gods" because they "continue."

Key wording

"continuation of the seeds forever and ever"

D&C 132:19; see also 132:20

Takeaway: godhood is connected to sealing, eternal marriage, and continuing posterity or increase.

Does not yet clearly teach

That exalted couples literally create spirit children, own worlds, or stand in an infinite backward chain of gods.

Development step

The doctrine moves from divine inheritance to generative divine family life. D&C 132 is the hinge text.

Apr 1844

King Follett Discourse: Godhood as Progression

Joseph sermon

In the King Follett discourse, Joseph Smith teaches that God was once as humans are now and that humans must learn how to become gods. He also teaches that the human spirit or intelligence is not created and has no beginning.

Key wording

"the Father was once on an earth like us"
"learn how to be a god yourself ... as all Gods has done"
"God never had power to create the spirit of man"

King Follett discourse, Woodruff and Clayton/Bullock accounts

Takeaway: godhood is not only an inherited status; it is a path of progression. God himself has traversed that path, while human spirits/intelligences are uncreated.

What remains ambiguous

"As all Gods has done" strongly teaches learned ascent. By itself, it does not settle whether Joseph is emphasizing horizontal entrance into a divine council, vertical divine ancestry, or both.

Development step

It radicalizes the doctrine: humans become like God because God was once like humans. The stronger upward-chain logic becomes clearer two months later in the Sermon in the Grove.

Jun 1844

Sermon in the Grove: Plurality and Upward Chain

Joseph sermon

In Joseph Smith's final major public doctrinal sermon, he argues for a plurality of gods. He reasons that if Jesus has a Father, the Father may also have a Father, and that intelligences exist one above another with no end.

Key wording

"intelligences exist one above another"
"where was there ever a Son witht. a Far."

Sermon in the Grove, Thomas Bullock report

Takeaway: this is the clearest Joseph Smith source for a hierarchy or chain of divine beings above the Father.

Still not fully systematized

It does not explain the mechanics of heavenly reproduction or reconcile them with uncreated intelligence.

Development step

The "infinite regress" idea is much stronger here than in D&C 132 or even King Follett.

1845-1890

Young, the Pratts, Snow, and Phelps: Heavenly Parents and Spirit Birth

later development

After Joseph Smith's death, Latter-day Saint thinkers increasingly connected exaltation, heavenly parents, plural marriage, and eternal procreation. Eliza R. Snow's "O My Father" gave beloved poetic form to Heavenly Mother. Orson and Parley Pratt and Brigham Young developed more explicit models in which exalted parents have spirit children and continue divine family work. Historian Jonathan Stapley describes this early successor theology as "viviparous spirit birth": spirits were created through celestial procreation using spirit element, not through Roberts's later uncreated-intelligence model.

Key wording

"spirits were begotten, born and educated"

Brigham Young, Journal of Discourses 18:258

"Spirits were created just as human babies were created, except with spirit element."

Jonathan Stapley, "Plans of Salvation"

Takeaway: a fuller spirit-birth and eternal-procreation theology appears in the post-Joseph polygamy era before Roberts's later tripartite reconciliation.

Not all equal authority

These teachings are historically important but not identical to canon; some were speculative or later deemphasized.

Development step

This is where "continuation of seeds" is commonly read as literal heavenly parenthood of spirit children. In Young's version, personhood begins with spirit birth from spirit element; in Pratt's version, intelligent atoms or spirit matter are organized differently.

1900s

B. H. Roberts: The Tripartite Reconciliation

system building

B. H. Roberts tries to reconcile Joseph's uncreated-spirit teaching with the already-existing Young/Pratt tradition of spirit birth. His solution distinguishes three stages: uncreated intelligence, spirit embodiment or spirit birth, and mortal embodiment.

Key wording

"Spirits are uncreated intelligences inhabiting spiritual bodies"

B. H. Roberts, Seventy's Course in Theology

Takeaway: spirit birth does not create the eternal self; it clothes or organizes an eternal intelligence into spirit embodiment. Roberts is not inventing spirit birth; he is reinterpreting it so the core person can remain uncreated.

Not canonized

Roberts's model became influential, but it is a later theological synthesis, not an explicit scriptural explanation.

Development step

Roberts shows that Joseph's uncreated-spirit teaching and the successors' viviparous spirit-birth theology had generated a real metaphysical problem that required careful reconciliation.

Today

Modern Official Framing: Divine Potential, Fewer Mechanics

current caution

Modern Church materials clearly teach that human beings are spirit children of heavenly parents and can become like Heavenly Father through Christ. They generally do not emphasize older speculative details about divine polygamy, spirit gestation, ownership of worlds, or the exact mechanics of infinite regress.

Key wording

"spirit children of heavenly parents"

Gospel Topics: Heavenly Parents

Takeaway: modern official materials teach divine parentage, divine potential, exaltation, and becoming like God.

Usually not specified

How spirit birth works, whether there is a literal infinite regress, and what exalted beings do in cosmic detail.

Development step

The modern public emphasis is devotional and relational more than speculative and cosmological.

What Often Gets Conflated

D&C 76

Becoming gods

Early and scriptural. The exalted inherit divine glory as children of God and joint-heirs with Christ.

D&C 132

Eternal increase

Scriptural, but underdetermined. It teaches "continuation of the seeds"; it does not spell out the biological or metaphysical mechanism.

KFD / SitG

God progressed; gods above gods

Joseph Smith sermon material, not canon. King Follett teaches God's progression; Sermon in the Grove gives the clearest upward-chain logic.

Young / Pratt / Roberts

Spirit birth and system building

Post-Joseph theology supplies the fuller heavenly-parent mechanism and later attempts to harmonize it with uncreated intelligence.

The Cleanest Thesis

Early Latter-day Saint deification theology was not revealed all at once. D&C 76 teaches that the exalted become gods as heirs of God through Christ. D&C 132 adds sealing, eternal marriage, and continuing posterity as the meaning of exalted godhood. King Follett then teaches that God himself progressed to godhood and that human spirits are uncreated. The Sermon in the Grove most clearly teaches a plurality or chain of gods. After Joseph's death, Brigham Young, the Pratts, Eliza R. Snow, and W. W. Phelps developed the fuller heavenly-parent and viviparous spirit-birth system many Latter-day Saints inherited. Later, B. H. Roberts tried to reconcile that inherited spirit-birth model with Joseph's teaching that spirits or intelligences are uncreated.

Beginner Q&A

Does D&C 76 already teach that humans can become gods?

Yes. It is one of the earliest and clearest scriptural texts for exalted humans being called gods. But it frames that godhood as inheritance through Christ, not as eternal marriage or spirit birth.

Does D&C 132 teach infinite regress?

Not by itself. It teaches godhood and eternal increase. The strongest textual meaning is continuing Abrahamic posterity through sealing. Infinite regress becomes much clearer in the Sermon in the Grove.

Does "continuation of the seeds" mean only living forever with your current family?

Probably not. The language of seed, continuation, enlargement, and Abrahamic posterity points to ongoing increase, not just preservation of a fixed family circle.

Does "continuation of the seeds" clearly mean literal spirit birth?

No. That became a major later interpretation, especially in Utah-era theology, but D&C 132 itself does not explain the mechanics.

Why does B. H. Roberts matter?

Because he saw the conflict between two inherited claims: human spirits/intelligences are uncreated, yet exalted parents have spirit children. Earlier leaders such as Brigham Young already taught a form of spirit birth using spirit element. Roberts's tripartite model tries to preserve spirit birth while saying it gives embodiment to an eternal intelligence rather than creating a new self from nothing.
